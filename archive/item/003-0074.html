Marine Heatwave and Extreme Hypoxia Coincide With Rapid Coral Bleaching and Mortality on a Central Red Sea Coral Reef

Shannon G. Klein, Eleonora Re, Naira Pluma, Cassandra Roch, Carlos M. Duarte
Ecology and Evolution · 2026-11-04 · doi:10.1002/ece3.74606
Coral reefMarineOther sensorsMonitoringEmpirical / fieldPeer reviewedCoralsin situ loggerssatellite SSTrepeated surveys#marine heatwave#hypoxia#compound extremes#Red Sea

In mid-2024 a severe marine heatwave hit a central Red Sea reef, and it coincided with repeated night-time low-oxygen events. As water passed 33°C, night oxygen minima fell to 15.2–57.8 µmol O₂ kg⁻¹, and the day-night temperature swing shrank to 0.31–0.67°C, so there was no cool reprieve at night.

🧂 Caveats: One reef and one event, with an observational design. The authors show the conditions coincided, but don't experimentally separate heat from hypoxia.
