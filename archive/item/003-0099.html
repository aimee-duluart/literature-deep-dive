Computational bioacoustics with deep learning: a review and roadmap

Dan Stowell
PeerJ · 2022-03-21 · doi:10.7717/peerj.13152
TerrestrialMarineFreshwaterAcousticsSpecies detectionMonitoringReviewPeer revieweddeep learningCNN#computational bioacoustics#roadmap

A single-author tour of deep learning for animal sound: which ideas were borrowed from speech and image processing, where bioacoustic data are genuinely different (rare calls, overlapping sources, shifting backgrounds), and a principled roadmap of open problems the community should tackle. Why it mattered: it's the review people hand to new PAM students, and it set the agenda (low-resource species, generalisation, richer tasks than presence/absence).

🧂 Caveats: A narrative review with a self-described “subjective but principled” roadmap, written before the foundation-model wave.

Catch-up area: Bioacoustics and PAM

Since then: Ghani, Denton, Kahl & Klinck (2023) showed embeddings from large birdsong models transfer to bats, marine mammals and amphibians better than general-audio embeddings, answering one of the roadmap's questions. doi:10.1038/s41598-023-49989-z (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-49989-z)

Reading level: Worth a full read
