Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science

Sayash Kapoor, Arvind Narayanan
Patterns · 2023-09 · doi:10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804
ReviewPeer reviewedliterature surveyreproducibility study#data leakage#reproducibility#model info sheets

Zooming out from ecology: a literature survey found leakage in 17 fields, affecting 294 papers, sometimes leading to wildly overoptimistic conclusions. The authors give a taxonomy of eight types of leakage, from textbook errors to open problems, and propose “model info sheets” to check for each. In a civil-war prediction case study, once errors were fixed, complex ML models did no better than decades-old logistic regression.

🧂 Caveats: It mostly covers social and biomedical fields; ecology appears only lightly, so the ecological examples are up to you.

Catch-up area: Transferable CV/ML: evaluation and generalisation

Since then: Kapoor et al. (2024) turned it into REFORMS, a consensus 32-question checklist for ML-based science from 19 researchers across disciplines. doi:10.1126/sciadv.adk3452 (https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adk3452)

Reading level: Worth a full read
