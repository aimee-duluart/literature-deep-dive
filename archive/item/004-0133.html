WILDS: A Benchmark of in-the-Wild Distribution Shifts

Pang Wei Koh, Shiori Sagawa, Henrik Marklund, Sang Michael Xie, Marvin Zhang, Akshay Balsubramani, Weihua Hu, Michihiro Yasunaga, Richard Lanas Phillips, Irena Gao, Tony Lee, Etienne David, Ian Stavness, Wei Guo, Berton A. Earnshaw, Imran S. Haque, Sara Beery, Jure Leskovec, Anshul Kundaje, Emma Pierson, Sergey Levine, Chelsea Finn, Percy Liang
ICML 2021 (PMLR 139) · 2021-07-01
TerrestrialVideo / imageryRemote sensingMultimodalDatasetPeer reviewedbenchmarkingdomain generalisation#distribution shift#iWildCam#robustness

The ML community took the hint. WILDS curated 10 datasets with real distribution shifts, including shifts across camera traps for wildlife monitoring (iWildCam), across hospitals and across time and place in satellite imagery. On every dataset, standard training gave substantially lower out-of-distribution than in-distribution performance, and existing robustness methods didn't close the gap.

🧂 Caveats: Benchmarks fix the shift in advance; your deployment shift (new reef, new season, new turbidity) may look nothing like theirs.

Catch-up area: Transferable CV/ML: evaluation and generalisation

Since then: WILDS 2.0 (Sagawa et al., ICLR 2022) added curated unlabelled data to 8 of the 10 datasets to test whether unlabelled deployment data can mitigate shift. arXiv:2112.05090 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05090)
