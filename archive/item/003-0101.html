Perspectives in machine learning for wildlife conservation

Devis Tuia, Benjamin Kellenberger, Sara Beery, Blair R. Costelloe, Silvia Zuffi, Benjamin Risse, Alexander Mathis, Mackenzie W. Mathis, Frank van Langevelde, Tilo Burghardt, Roland Kays, Holger Klinck, Martin Wikelski, Iain D. Couzin, Grant van Horn, Margaret C. Crofoot, Charles V. Stewart, Tanya Berger-Wolf
Nature Communications · 2022-02-09 · doi:10.1038/s41467-022-27980-y
TerrestrialMarineMultimodalReviewPeer reviewedmachine learninghybrid modelling#wildlife conservation#sensors#interdisciplinarity

An 18-author perspective from ecologists and CV researchers: cheap sensors now produce more data than ecologists can process, and the way forward is ML combined with domain knowledge, feeding better inputs into ecological models and, eventually, hybrid ML–ecology models. It also argues for close interdisciplinary teams and training a new generation of ecology data scientists.

🧂 Caveats: A perspective, not new results; the examples skew terrestrial and the field has since moved fast towards foundation models.

Catch-up area: Emerging tools: ML for conservation

Since then: BioCLIP (Stevens et al., CVPR 2024) trained a vision foundation model on the TreeOfLife-10M dataset, an early step towards the general-purpose biology models the perspective anticipated. arXiv:2311.18803 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18803)
