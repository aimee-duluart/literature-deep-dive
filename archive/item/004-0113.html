Steroid hormones produced by coral-associated Endozoicomonas influence coral immune response during thermal stress

Amin R. Mohamed, Michael A. Ochsenkühn, Trent D. Haydon, Salah Abdelrazig, Lisa S. Y. Coe, Gabriel Mochales-Riaño, Woonkee S. Jo, Alan R. Healy, David Abrego, Jean-Baptiste Raina, John A. Burt, Shady A. Amin
Nature Microbiology · 2026-10-22 · doi:10.1038/s41564-026-02494-1
Coral reefMarineOther sensorsInteraction classificationEmpirical / labPeer reviewedCoralsSymbiontsmulti-omicsculture experimentstranscriptomics#host-microbe symbiosis#steroid hormones#coral immunity#Arabian Gulf

Mohamed … Amin found that Endozoicomonas can convert cholesterol into testosterone and progesterone, and that giving corals these hormones during lab heat stress reprogrammed their innate immune response. It's a symbiosis written in chemistry, not behaviour, but still a species interaction. doi:10.1038/s41564-026-02494-1
