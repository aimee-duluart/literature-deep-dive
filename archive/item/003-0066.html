Automated identification of individual birds by song enables multi-year recapture from passive acoustic monitoring data

Sam Lapp, R. Patrick Lyon, Scott J. Wilson, Tessa A. Rhinehart, Chapin Czarnecki, Lauren M. Chronister, Cameron J. Fiss, Jeffery L. Larkin, Erin Bayne, Justin Kitzes
Nature Methods · 2026-11-05 · doi:10.1038/s41592-026-03303-7
TerrestrialAcousticsMonitoringSpecies detectionMethods / toolPeer reviewedBirdsCNNclusteringdimensionality reductionindividual identification#capture-recapture#individual ID#passive acoustic monitoring#survival

Camera traps and recorders usually stop at species. Tracking individuals still means capture-mark-recapture.

🧂 Caveats: One species with a stereotyped, individually distinctive song, and territorial males that stay put. Species with variable or shared repertoires, or that move a lot, will be harder. Clustering errors (one bird split in two, or two merged) feed straight into survival estimates, so check the paper's error analysis before you borrow the numbers.
