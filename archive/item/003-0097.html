Automatically identifying, counting, and describing wild animals in camera-trap images with deep learning

Mohammad Sadegh Norouzzadeh, Anh Nguyen, Margaret Kosmala, Alexandra Swanson, Meredith S. Palmer, Craig Packer, Jeff Clune
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences · 2018-06-05 · doi:10.1073/pnas.1719367115
TerrestrialVideo / imagerySpecies detectionBehaviour classificationMethods / toolPeer reviewedMammalsCNNconfidence thresholding#camera traps#Snapshot Serengeti

Deep CNNs trained on the 3.2-million-image Snapshot Serengeti set identified 48 species with >93.8% accuracy and also counted animals and described behaviours. The clever bit: by only auto-labelling images the network was confident about, it handled 99.3% of the data at the same 96.6% accuracy as the crowdsourced volunteers, saving an estimated 8+ years of human labelling.

🧂 Caveats: One savanna dataset with plenty of labels per common species; rare species and new sites are much harder, which the later literature hammered home.

Catch-up area: Transferable CV/ML: camera traps

Since then: Beery, Morris & Yang's MegaDetector pipeline (2019) split the job into a generic “animal / person / vehicle” detector plus local classifiers, and became the go-to camera-trap tool. arXiv:1907.06772 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06772)
