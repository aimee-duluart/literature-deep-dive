Cross‐validation strategies for data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or phylogenetic structure

David R. Roberts, Volker Bahn, Simone Ciuti, Mark S. Boyce, Jane Elith, Gurutzeta Guillera‐Arroita, Severin Hauenstein, José J. Lahoz‐Monfort, Boris Schröder, Wilfried Thuiller, David I. Warton, Brendan A. Wintle, Florian Hartig, Carsten F. Dormann
Ecography · 2017-03-03 · doi:10.1111/ecog.02881
TerrestrialMarineFreshwaterReviewPeer reviewedblock cross-validationsimulation#cross-validation#dependence structure#model evaluation

Reviewing the ecological literature, with simulations and case studies, the authors show that ignoring dependence (in space, time, groups or phylogeny) when cross-validating seriously underestimates predictive error and lets non-causal predictors look useful. Block cross-validation was nearly always more appropriate than random CV when the goal is predicting to new data.

🧂 Caveats: Mostly SDM-style regression examples; choosing block size and blocking variable is still a judgement call.

Catch-up area: Transferable CV/ML: evaluation and generalisation

Since then: Ploton et al. (2020) showed the stakes: a forest-biomass model that looked like it explained over half the variation under random validation had quasi-null predictive power under spatial validation. doi:10.1038/s41467-020-18321-y (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18321-y)
