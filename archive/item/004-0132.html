Recognition in Terra Incognita

Sara Beery, Grant Van Horn, Pietro Perona
ECCV 2018 (Lecture Notes in Computer Science) · 2018-10-06 · doi:10.1007/978-3-030-01270-0_28
TerrestrialVideo / imagerySpecies detectionDatasetPeer reviewedMammalsobject detectionclassificationdomain generalisation#camera traps#new locations#distribution shift

Images from 20 camera traps, with a simple challenge: train at a handful of locations, then detect and classify animals at new locations with no training data. State-of-the-art models did very well at the cameras they were trained on, but generalised poorly to new ones, especially for classification. Camera traps are fixed, so the background barely changes, and models learn it.

🧂 Caveats: Small by today's standards (20 cameras, American Southwest), and CNNs from 2018. Modern foundation models narrow the gap but don't close it.

Catch-up area: Transferable CV/ML: camera traps and domain shift

Since then: Beery et al.'s Context R-CNN (2020) turned the fixed background into an asset, using a per-camera memory of unlabelled frames to boost detection at new cameras. arXiv:1912.03538 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03538)

Reading level: Read the methods
